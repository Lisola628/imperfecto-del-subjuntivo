Monday, November 16 · Capítulo 18, Gramática 48

Del pretérito al imperfecto de subjuntivo

Before we meet the past subjunctive, we go back to a tense you already own: the preterite. One single form of it — the ellos form — is the key to everything new this week. If you can say votaron, comieron and fueron, you are most of the way there.

Today you will be able to…

  • Rebuild the preterite: regular endings, stem changes, and the irregular verbs.
  • Talk about what happened in the news and in the last elections.
  • Build the past subjunctive from the ellos form: -ron → -ra.
  • Say what someone wanted, asked for, or doubted in the past.

Bring to class: your book (Gramática 48) and one index card.

Repaso del pretérito: las formas regulares

¡OJO!

The accent is not decoration: voto is “I vote” and votó is “he/she/you voted.”

Verbs in -car, -gar, -zar change their spelling in the yo form only: busqué, llegué, empecé.

Paso 2 · 10 min

Los rebeldes: la forma de ellos

Pairs. One of you reads the infinitive; the other says the ellos form out loud before anyone writes. Switch roles halfway down.

Four families to remember

  • -ir stem-changing verbs change e → i or o → u, only in the third-person forms: servir → sirvió, sirvieron.
  • Vowel + -er/-ir turns the i into y: creer → creyó, creyeron.
  • Irregular stems share one set of endings with no accents (-e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron): estuv-, tuv-, pud-, pus-, sup-, quis-, hic-, vin-. Stems ending in j drop the i: dij-, traj- → -eron.
  • Totally irregular: ser and ir share one preterite; dar and ver take -er/-ir endings without accents.

Paso 4 · 9 min

El truco: -ron → -ra

We do the first row together. Finish the rest with your partner, saying each form out loud.

The past subjunctive (el imperfecto de subjuntivo) has no stems of its own. It borrows everything from the preterite. Three moves: take the ellos form, drop -ron, add the new endings. Every irregularity you practiced in Paso 2 comes along for the ride.

votaron → vota- → votara  ·  comieron → comie- → comiera  ·  fueron → fue- → fuera
Las terminaciones: -ra, -ras, -ra, -ramos, -rais, -ran

Infinitivoellos (pretérito)que yo / usted…que nosotros…
votarvotaronvotaravotáramos
comer

¡OJO!

The nosotros form always carries a written accent: votáramos, comiéramos, fuéramos.

The yo form and the usted / él / ella form are identical. Context, or a subject pronoun, tells you who.

You may also see a second set of endings in readings: votase, comiese, fuese. Same meaning. Recognize it; you do not need to produce it.

Paso 5 · 9 min

Primeras oraciones en el pasado

Pairs for part A. Part B is yours first, then share it.

Nothing changes about when you need the subjunctive — the same triggers from Capítulo 15 still decide it. What changes is the time: when the main clause is in the past, the second clause takes the past subjunctive.

HoyEn el pasado
El presidente quiere que los ciudadanos voten.El presidente quería que los ciudadanos votaran.

A. Complete.

  1. Los reporteros querían que el candidato (decir) la verdad.
  2. Era importante que todos los ciudadanos (votar).
  3. Yo dudaba que la prensa (informar) bien sobre la huelga.
  4. Mis padres me pidieron que (leer) las noticias todos los días.
  5. Nos alegramos de que la guerra (terminar).
  6. El gobierno prohibió que los obreros (hacer) huelga.

B. Cuando yo era niño/a… Write three true sentences. Then read them to your partner, who asks: «¿Y usted lo hizo?» Answer in the preterite.

Cuando yo era niño/a, mis padres querían que yo… · mis maestros me pedían que… · mis abuelos esperaban que…

Wednesday, November 18 · Capítulo 18, Gramática 48

El imperfecto de subjuntivo: usos y deseos imposibles

On Monday you built the forms. Today you put them to work: reporting what people wanted, feared and doubted in the past — and wishing for things that are not true right now.

Today you will be able to…

  • Produce past subjunctive forms quickly from the ellos preterite.
  • Move a sentence from the present to the past: quiere que vote → quería que votara.
  • Choose between indicative and past subjunctive by looking at the main clause.
  • Express impossible wishes with Ojalá and polite requests with quisiera.

Bring to class: your book (Gramática 48), Monday’s page, and one index card.

Paso 2 · 8 min

Del presente al pasado

Pairs. The beginning of each new sentence is given. Change the second verb to match.

The rule in one line: main clause in the present → present subjunctive. Main clause in the past (preterite or imperfect) → past subjunctive.

Modelo: Quiero que usted lea el periódico. → Quería que usted leyera el periódico.

  1. El presidente quiere que los ciudadanos se enteren de la tragedia.
    El presidente quería que
  2. Es necesario que la prensa informe sin censura.
    Era necesario que
  3. Dudo que los políticos digan la verdad.
    Dudaba que
  4. Los obreros piden que el gobierno cambie la ley.
    Los obreros pidieron que
  5. Me alegro de que haya paz.
    Me alegré de que

¡OJO! Number 5: hay comes from haber. Its past subjunctive is hubiera (from hubieron), and like hay, it does not change for singular or plural: Dudaba que hubiera más de cien personas en la protesta.

Paso 3 · 10 min

¿Indicativo o subjuntivo? En el pasado

Pairs, in this order: read only the main clause, say out loud which mood you need, and then write. Not every blank is subjunctive.

The same three situations as always

  • Influence, emotion, doubt and denial in the main clause.
  • A person or thing that is unknown or may not exist.
  • Conjunctions of purpose and condition: para que, antes de que, sin que, a menos que, con tal de que.
  1. Era verdad que el candidato (ganar) las elecciones.
  2. No creíamos que el dictador (dejar) el poder.
  3. Buscaban un testigo que (ver) el choque.
  4. Conocían a un testigo que (ver) el choque.
  5. Los ciudadanos votaron para que (haber) un cambio.
  6. La reportera salió antes de que (terminar) el discurso.
  7. Sabíamos que la huelga (durar) tres semanas.
  8. Temían que nosotros no (poder) votar.

¡OJO! Doubt still takes the subjunctive in the past. Dudaba que and no creía que + past subjunctive; sabía que and era verdad que + indicative.

Check yourself: numbers 3 and 4 use the same verb and the same crash. Why are the answers different? English is fine.

Paso 5 · 8 min

Ojalá: deseos imposibles

Write on your own (3 min). Then groups of three: read your wishes, and choose the best one from the group to share with the class.

Ojalá changes meaning with the tense that follows it. With the present subjunctive, the wish could still come true. With the past subjunctive, you are wishing for something that is not true now, or is very unlikely.

Quisiera…: para pedir con cortesía

Pairs. You are reporters for the campus newspaper and you need an interview with the mayor of Baton Rouge.

The past subjunctive of querer softens a request. It is the polite way to ask for something in a café, an office, or an interview.

Quisiera un café, por favor.  ·  Quisiéramos hablar con usted un momento.

  2. Quisiera
